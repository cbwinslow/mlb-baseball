# Metric catalog triage backlog (2026-09-23)

## What happened

151 metric YAML entries existed under this directory. ~135 of them were
generated by an external AI agent, asked to implement metrics from Tom
Tango's *The Book*, sabermetrics literature, Retrosheet writeups, and
academic papers. The owner did not trust the batch and asked for a full
triage: keep what's real, research and fix what's cheap to fix, delete the
rest.

Every entry was checked against two things: (1) is it actually wired to
real `gold`/`core`/`raw` data (not just a standalone CLI calculator taking
hand-typed numbers), and (2) is its citation real and verifiable (not an
invented acronym or a made-up constant).

Result: **43 kept as-is** (already real, wired, tested), **4 kept for a
near-term real fix** (`arm_slot_engine`, `babip_luck_scanner`,
`pitch_tunneling_engine`, `season_monte_carlo_simulation` — genuine
published formulas or a one-line engineering fix exist), **3 kept
unmodified after discovery that their code is wired into the separate
internal "Engine" serving layer** (`pipeline.py`/`api.py`/`daemon.py`/
`visual.py`: `stuff_plus_rating_engine`, plus `batted_ball_ballistic_distance`
and `strike_zone_kde_density` which share `model/heatmap.py` with it), and
**101 deleted** (yaml + `.py` implementation + dead CLI subcommand + dead
`doctor.py` health-check registration + dedicated test file) — 8 of those
101 had literally invented acronyms/constants with no real basis found
anywhere in the literature, the other 93 were disconnected CLI-only
calculators.

Of the 101 deleted, **13 had a real, legitimate underlying concept** —
researched against FanGraphs Library, Baseball Prospectus, Baseball
Savant/Statcast methodology pages, SABR/Baseball Research Journal, and
academic papers (2026-09-23) — but no swappable published formula exists,
so building them for real is a genuine modeling task, not a documentation
fix. They're recorded here so the ideas and their real citations aren't
lost, and so nobody re-discovers "hey, catcher blocking runs saved is a
real Statcast metric" from scratch.

## Backlog: real concept, no cheap fix

Each of these needs an actual small research/derivation project before it
can re-enter the catalog with `status: published` or better — not just a
constant swap.

- **`aging_projection_engine`** — real concept: Tango/Lichtman/Dolphin's
  "delta method" for aging curves. Citation: Tom Tango, Mitchel Lichtman,
  Andrew Dolphin, *The Book: Playing the Percentages in Baseball* (2006);
  FanGraphs Library, "The Beginner's Guide to Aging Curves"
  (library.fangraphs.com/the-beginners-guide-to-aging-curves).

- **`blast_angle_engine`** — real concept: launch-angle "tightness"
  (standard deviation of launch angle). Citation: FanGraphs RotoGraphs,
  "Let's Talk About Launch Angle 'Tightness'"
  (fantasy.fangraphs.com/lets-talk-about-launch-angle-tightness). The
  project's "LWTS/BASD" acronyms and run-surplus formula are not real —
  only the underlying tightness concept is.

- **`block_suppression_engine`** — real, current, well-known Statcast
  metric exists (Catcher Blocking / Blocks Above Average, ≈0.25 runs per
  block above average, a per-pitch probability model on
  location/speed/movement/catcher position/handedness). Citation: Baseball
  Savant, Catcher Blocking leaderboard methodology
  (baseballsavant.mlb.com/leaderboard/catcher-blocking). The project's flat
  88%/0.85s/75% thresholds bear no resemblance to the real per-pitch model
  — this was the most misleading entry in the batch, not just uncited.

- **`bullpen_optimizer_engine`** — component concepts (FIP, platoon
  splits, rest/workload effects) are each real and separately citable, but
  no published combined "optimizer" formula exists. Citations: Tom Tango
  et al., *The Book* (platoon splits); FanGraphs Library (FIP).

- **`bunt_defense_engine`** — real concept: "Bunt Runs Saved" (rBU), a
  component of Defensive Runs Saved for 1B/3B/C/P. Citation: Baseball Info
  Solutions / The Fielding Bible, "Team Defensive Runs Saved"
  (archive.fieldingbible.com); SABR, "Measuring Defense: Entering the Zones
  of Fielding Statistics." Proprietary/BIS-derived constants, not published
  as swappable coefficients.

- **`catcher_blocking_engine`** — real concept: Runs per Passed Pitch
  (RPP), a per-pitch passed-ball/wild-pitch probability model. Citation:
  FanGraphs adopted Bojan Koprivica's model; FanGraphs Library, "Catcher
  Defense"; FanGraphs blog, "Catcher Pitch Blocking & WAR Update"
  (blogs.fangraphs.com). Proprietary constants, not published as swappable
  coefficients.

- **`first_pitch_ambush_engine`** — first-pitch swing/take run value is
  real (Statcast's swing/take run-value leaderboard), but no published
  composite "ambush" index exists. Citations: Baseball Savant, "Swing/Take
  Run Value Leaderboard"; The Hardball Times, "Swinging at First Pitch and
  Game Theory." The project's "FPAV/FPSV" acronyms are invented.

- **`first_step_burst_engine`** — closest real analog of the batch:
  Statcast's "Jump" metric (Reaction + Burst + Route, each a measured
  distance/time component) maps almost 1:1 onto this engine's
  reaction/distance/efficiency structure, just in different units with
  unpublished league-average constants. Citation: MLB.com Glossary, "Jump"
  (mlb.com/glossary/statcast/jump); FanGraphs, "Learning From Statcast's
  Outfield Jump Metrics"; baseballsavant.com/leaderboard/outfield_jump. The
  project's "FSRJI" acronym is invented.

- **`outfield_arm_accuracy_engine`** — real concept: Outfield Arm Runs /
  "Arm Value" (includes accuracy and a runner-holds/freeze concept).
  Citations: MLB.com Glossary, "Arm Strength" (explicitly notes accuracy
  matters beyond velocity); FanGraphs UZR "ARM" component
  (library.fangraphs.com/defense/uzr); FanGraphs Community Blog, "The Arm
  of Marcell Ozuna and the Outfield Arm Runs Saved Statistic." The
  project's "ASI/RFSV" acronyms are invented; the real UZR ARM constants
  are proprietary, not published as swappable coefficients.

- **`outfield_arm_engine`** — partial real basis: Statcast "Arm Strength,"
  "Extra Bases Taken Run Value," and "Fielding Run Value" are real, but
  they're fitted probability models on tracked throw/runner data, not
  closed-form formulas. Citations: MLB.com Glossary, "Arm Strength"
  (mlb.com/glossary/statcast/arm-strength); Baseball Savant, "Extra Bases
  Taken" and "Fielding Run Value" leaderboards
  (baseballsavant.mlb.com/leaderboard/fielding-run-value).

- **`outfield_catch_probability_engine`** — real and public: Statcast's
  "Catch Probability" and its 5-star bucket system. Citations: Baseball
  Savant, "Statcast Catch Probability"
  (baseballsavant.mlb.com/statcast_catch_probability); MLB Glossary, "Catch
  Probability." The module's star-tier boundaries are a legitimately
  correct match to the real thing — only the underlying per-play
  probability model itself needs real (fitted-model) work, not the tiers.

- **`pitcher_workload_fatigue_engine`** — the acute(7-day):chronic(28-day)
  workload ratio (ACWR) core is a real, published sports-science framework
  with baseball-specific applications. Citations: Gabbett, "The
  training-injury prevention paradox," *British Journal of Sports Medicine*
  (2016); Zaremski et al., "Workload Risk Factors for Pitching-Related
  Injuries in High School Baseball Pitchers" (2024,
  doi.org/10.1177/03635465241246559). The project's composite "Fatigue
  Risk Index" (velocity-decay threshold, penalty weights, tier cutoffs) has
  no published source anywhere — only the ACWR core is citable.

- **`xslg_power_engine`** — real and public: Baseball Savant's xSLG, a
  fitted nearest-neighbor-style model on individual batted-ball exit
  velocity/launch angle (and sprint speed for some batted-ball types), not
  a linear combination of category counts. Citation: Baseball Savant,
  "Statcast Expected wOBA, xBA, xSLG"
  (baseballsavant.mlb.com/expected_statistics). The project's per-category
  weights (2.50/1.25/0.65/...) don't and structurally can't cheaply match
  the real per-batted-ball model.

## Confirmed fabricated (no real basis found, cut, not backlogged)

`arm_alignment_engine` ("AAAR"), `air_trap_engine`, `bullpen_leverage_volatility_engine`,
`exp_resist_engine` ("TERI"), `foul_attrition_engine` ("BFAI/SRAR"),
`heat_check_engine` ("IZHSMI"), `spray_direction_engine` (invented "2.2
scaling constant"), `sweetspot_contact_engine` ("Ideal Contact Rate" —
Sweet-Spot% and Hard-Hit% inputs are real Statcast terms, the blended
composite is not). These acronyms/composites do not appear anywhere in
sabermetrics literature; no follow-up is recommended beyond what's already
captured here.
